If your work only exists inside someone else's cloud dashboard, you are renting your own output.

## What's happening

Platforms change pricing tiers, sunset features, alter terms of service, or lock exports behind paywalls. Most people don't notice how dependent their daily workflow has become until a service goes down or an account gets flagged without warning.

Your writing, project documentation, and configuration files shouldn't be trapped behind a proprietary login screen.

## What local-first ownership looks like

Moving back to file-based workflows doesn't mean giving up convenience — it means keeping the master copy on your own disk:

- **Your files live on your machine first**: cloud sync or Git remotes act as backups and publishing targets, not single points of failure.
- **Standard formats everywhere**: Markdown for writing, YAML/JSON for config, plain directories for media.
- **Offline resilience**: you can write, edit, and test your entire site without an internet connection.

## Where we stand

1. **No proprietary lock-in for our own publishing.** Everything we publish starts as a local text file tracked in Git.
2. **Transparent workflows.** Whether editing locally or pushing changes live, every update is visible and reversible.
3. **Tools serve the files, never the reverse.** Editors and CMS interfaces can change anytime without touching the underlying content.

## What you can do today

Take ten minutes to check where your most important notes, posts, or project configs live. If you can't open them in a basic text editor tomorrow without an internet connection, it might be time to move them into plain files you actually own.
