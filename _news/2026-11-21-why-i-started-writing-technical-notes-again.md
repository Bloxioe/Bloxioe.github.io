Most of the things I figure out while debugging at 2 AM used to vanish into closed terminal tabs or scattered chat messages.

## What changed

Short posts on social platforms are fine for quick announcements, but they are terrible for anything you want to find six months later. Links rot, algorithms bury older updates, and formatting gets stripped down to fit a feed.

Having a dedicated space where every note lives as a clean Markdown file changes how you think about what you build.

## What writing here is for

This blog isn't meant to chase trends or publish on a rigid schedule. It exists for three practical reasons:

- **A searchable log**: documenting fixes, setups, and architecture decisions so future-me doesn't have to solve the same problem twice.
- **Clear positions**: stating where we stand on open software, user ownership, and web standards without character limits.
- **Project context**: giving updates and releases a permanent URL that anyone can bookmark or reference.

## Keeping it simple

1. **Write when there is something real to say.** No filler posts just to fill a calendar.
2. **Keep every post self-contained.** Clear headings, honest trade-offs, and working examples.
3. **Own the archive.** Plain text files in Git that stay readable decades from now.

If even one note here saves someone an hour of troubleshooting, it has done its job.
