The hacker who leaked early footage of Grand Theft Auto VI has finally been sentenced, and the outcome is going to surprise a lot of people. He got **seven years in prison**, yet he left custody on licence on the very same day.

The case has been running for more than four years, so here is a plain-English recap of what happened.

## Who is Arion Kurtaj?

Arion Kurtaj is 21 years old and used to live in Oxfordshire, England. He was part of the Lapsus$ hacking group, which became notorious in 2021 and 2022 for breaking into big companies and then bragging about it online.

He pleaded guilty to six charges, including fraud and computer misuse offences, at Woolwich Crown Court on 16 November. City of London Police confirmed the sentence this week.

## How seven years was calculated

The court first set a **nine-year sentence**. It then took two years off to account for the time he had already spent under a hospital order, which leaves seven. Because of that, he was released on licence on the day of sentencing rather than being sent back to a cell.

## What he actually did

The most shocking part of this story is *when* the biggest attack happened. In September 2022, Kurtaj was already on bail and had been placed in a hotel for his own protection. According to the Crown Prosecution Service, he used the following to get back into company systems from that room:

- **A mobile phone**
- **The hotel TV's Wi-Fi**
- **An Amazon Fire Stick**

From there he targeted Rockstar Games, the studio behind GTA. He stole about **90 gameplay videos** of the unreleased game and posted them online under the name "TeaPotUberHacker". He also threatened to release the game's source code, which prosecutors described as an attempt to blackmail the company.

## Other companies he hit

Rockstar was not the only victim. Prosecutors say Kurtaj was also involved in attacks on:

- **Uber** — data was stolen and deleted, and the attack cost the company more than **$2.8 million**
- **Revolut** — data belonging to thousands of customers was compromised
- **BT/EE** — he gained remote access in 2021, and the CPS linked it to a rise in unauthorised SIM swaps at the telecom provider

Police said the hacks covered a period from August 2020 to September 2022.

## A case that took four years

The path to this sentence was far from simple:

1. **September 2022** — Kurtaj is first arrested
2. **2023** — Doctors find him unfit to stand trial. A jury still decides he carried out the hacks, and he receives an indefinite hospital order
3. **January 2026** — A court rules the hospital order can be lifted, and the original charges are listed for trial
4. **March 2026** — While on remand, he is caught with a mobile phone in prison, which prosecutors say he used to send a mocking message to the emergency number 999
5. **November 2026** — He pleads guilty and is sentenced

## What police said

Detective Inspector Mike O'Sullivan of the City of London Police Cyber Crime Unit said the case shows how young people can be pulled into dangerous corners of the internet. He was also clear that Kurtaj knew exactly what he was doing and only admitted it in the end.

O'Sullivan also thanked the affected companies for working with investigators, saying that cooperation may have stopped other organisations from becoming victims.

## Why this case matters

A few things stand out beyond the headline:

- **Cybercrime has no borders**, but police are increasingly working with foreign agencies and private companies to track offenders down
- **Even big names are vulnerable** — Rockstar, Uber and Revolut all had their systems breached
- **Bail and monitoring are tricky** when someone can cause serious damage with nothing more than a phone and a hotel TV

## Final thoughts

For gamers, the leak was a shock back in 2022, and the story is only now reaching its legal conclusion. It is a good reminder that digital crimes carry real consequences, even if the final outcome looks unusual on paper.
